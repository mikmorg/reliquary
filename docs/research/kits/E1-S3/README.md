# Kit E1-S3: interview pilot, the owner vs a neutral facilitator

- **Spike:** E1-S3 "Interview pilot: owner vs neutral facilitator" (workstream E1, see
  `docs/research/PLAN.md`)
- **Exec tag:** FM
- **Prepared by / date:** spike runner (agent), 2026-09-29
- **Who runs it:** the owner runs some interviews and a neutral facilitator runs others (H5 L27;
  intake Q-F9). The relatives are the participants.
- **Time needed (planning estimate; not measured):**
  - briefing the facilitator: about 1 h, plus one practice interview;
  - each interview: sections 1–8 of the guide, about 45–60 min inside the visit;
  - coding: about 20 min per interview.
- **Data-handling class:** `FAM → AGG`. Notes, and recordings if consented, stay in the private
  store. Only story counts and themes leave.

## Purpose

Find out whether a neutral facilitator draws out more real, specific stories from relatives than
the owner does. The owner is family and will be their admin, so relatives may tell him less.

## Hypothesis

Interviews run by the facilitator yield **at least as many** concrete past-behaviour stories per
interview as interviews run by the owner.

## Decision it informs

| If the result is… | Then… |
|---|---|
| **Pass** (PLAN): the facilitator surfaces at least as many concrete past-behaviour stories | Keep the facilitator for the remaining visits where practical, at least for section 6 (admin access). Feeds the E2 test plan and the E1 bias note. |
| **Fail**: the facilitator surfaces fewer | The owner runs the remaining interviews. The E1 note records the owner-as-researcher bias and its controls (E1 note §5.2). |
| **No result**: no facilitator available, or fewer than 2 interviews each | The owner runs everything (H5 L27 fallback), and the bias is recorded. |

This is a small pilot, 2–3 interviews each. It shows direction, not statistical proof. Report the
counts, not a significance test.

## Before you start

- [ ] OD-21 (H3 rules) and the consent texts are approved by the owner.
- [ ] The facilitator is briefed on:
  - H3 §1–§4 (no names, no FAM in any tool; notes stay on paper or in the private store);
  - the interview guide;
  - how to mark ★ stories;
  - never to pitch or reassure.
- [ ] One practice interview run with the owner playing a relative. It is not counted.
- [ ] Assignment decided **before** the visits. Alternate facilitator and owner along E2's visit
      order, so that each gets a mix of typical and extreme users. Do not let the owner pick
      "easy" relatives for himself. Write the assignment down first.

## Procedure

1. At each assigned visit, the interviewer reads the consent texts and records the answers.
   **You should see:** ticked boxes and a signature, or a clear no. A no means stop the interview;
   the rest of the visit is still optional.
2. Run sections 1–8 of `interview-guide.md`. In the margin, mark each **candidate** story with ★
   while it is told.
   - If the facilitator runs section 6, the owner leaves the room.
3. Straight after the visit, the interviewer writes up the notes on paper or in the private store:
   the *kind* of thing, never names.
4. **Coding (blind swap):** each interviewer's notes are coded by the **other** person, using the
   definition below. The coder does not change the notes; they only count.
   - If a recording exists, the coder may listen to check a story, in the private store only.
5. For each interview, the coder fills in the coding sheet. **You should see:** one row per
   interview, with story counts by topic.
6. Where the two people disagree on whether something is a story, they discuss it and record the
   agreed count **and** how many items were disputed.
7. After the last pilot interview, add up the counts per interviewer (owner vs facilitator) and
   fill in the Results.

**Definition: a concrete past-behaviour story** is an account of **one specific episode that
really happened** to the speaker, with at least three of these four parts:

1. **when** it happened (even roughly: "last Christmas", "when I got this phone");
2. **what** happened (the trigger: lost, broken, full, changed phones, looked for a photo);
3. **what they did** (an action they took, or chose not to take);
4. **how it ended** (the outcome).

These do **not** count:

- general habits ("I usually …");
- opinions ("I think backups are …");
- hypotheticals ("I would …");
- another person's story told second-hand, unless the speaker took part.

**Stop and record "No result" if:** the relative is uncomfortable with the facilitator, or asks
for the owner to stay. Their wish wins.

## Cleaning up

1. Delete recordings within 90 days of the AGG being accepted (H3 R7), or sooner if the person asks.
2. Paper notes: keep them with the private-store papers. Shred them on the same schedule.

## Data handling

H3 defines the classes, rules and output check (`docs/research/h3-research-data-governance.md`
§2–§4; draft until OD-21).

- **Collected:** interview notes (kinds of things, not names), optional audio, and coding counts.
- **Never collected:** names of files, people or places, account details. Recordings never go into
  an AI or cloud transcription tool (H3 §1).
- **Raw data stays in** the private store.
- **May be pasted below:** counts per interviewer (O vs F), numbers of interviews, disputed-item
  counts, and themes phrased so that no one can be recognised. Any per-person cell below 5 is
  shown as "<5".
