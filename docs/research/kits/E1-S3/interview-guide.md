# E1 interview guide (semi-structured; used at every visit)

The owner and the neutral facilitator use the same guide, so that E1-S3 compares like with like.

## How to interview

- **Ask about the past and about specifics, not about the future.** Say "Tell me about the last
  time you changed phones", not "Would you use an app that…". Specific past episodes are the
  evidence this research needs, and E1-S3 counts them.
  - This is the central advice usually credited to Rob Fitzpatrick's *The Mom Test*. The book was
    not re-read for this kit, so the attribution is secondary.
- **Don't pitch.** Don't describe Reliquary, don't say it will fix anything, and don't correct
  beliefs during the interview. Note them; the check in section 4 comes later.
- **Follow a story with "And then what happened?", "When was that?", "What did you do?"** Stay with
  one story until it is concrete (when, what, what they did, how it ended).
- **Silence is fine.** Wait before asking the next question.
- **Never write down** file, album, folder or place names, account names or e-mails. Write the
  *kind* of thing ("wedding photos", "tax papers").
- The owner is family. Relatives may play down problems to be kind. Expect it, and ask for
  examples rather than ratings.
- Mark each **concrete past-behaviour story** in the margin with ★. That is the E1-S3 count; see
  `README.md` for the definition.

## 1. Warm-up and devices (5 min)

1. "Which phones, tablets and computers do you use, and which one do you use most?"
2. "Does anyone else use any of them? Do you use anyone else's?"
3. "Do you have a phone or computer from work or school?" (If yes: "Is anything personal on it,
   such as photos?" Self-report only; never run anything on it.)

## 2. Photos and videos today (5 min)

4. "When you take a photo, what usually happens to it afterwards?"
5. "Tell me about the last time you looked for an old photo. What happened?" ★
6. "Do you print photos or make albums? When did you last do that?"

## 3. Where keepsakes live (10 min)

Prompt list: go through it slowly and tick what applies on the person page (P9). It follows the
Library of Congress personal-archiving advice to look across cameras, computers, memory cards and
the web; that source was seen only as search snippets.

7. "Apart from your phone, where else are there photos, videos or important papers of yours?"
   Then prompt, one at a time: old phones; old computers; external drives or USB sticks; camera
   memory cards; CDs or DVDs; video tapes; prints, slides or albums; e-mail attachments; WhatsApp
   or other messaging apps; Facebook or other social media; scanned documents; voice recordings;
   a family tree program.
8. "Which of these would you most hate to lose?" (the kind of thing only)
9. "Is there anything important that exists in **only one place** right now?" (yes/no and the
   kind of thing)

**Write down the list of places mentioned** before the census runs (E1-S1 Part C step 18 compares
against it).

## 4. What they believe is safe (5 min)

10. "If this phone fell in a lake today, what would you lose? What would come back?"
11. "Is anything backed up at the moment? How do you know?"
12. "Do you pay for any storage, like iCloud or Google One? Roughly how much?"

Afterwards, in the device walk-through, **check together** in Settings (device checklist D11).
Record belief and reality separately (P7, P8). Don't comment on any gap while interviewing.

## 5. Past losses and changes (5 min)

13. "Have you ever lost photos or files? Tell me what happened." ★ (Ask for when, what was
    lost, what they tried, and how it ended.)
14. "Tell me about the last time you changed phones or computers. How did your photos get across?" ★
15. "Has a phone or computer ever told you it was full? What did you do?" ★

## 6. Admin access and exclusions (after consent has explained the trust model; 5–10 min)

If a neutral facilitator is running the interview, **the owner leaves the room** for this section.

Read first: "In the planned system, [Owner] could technically see every photo that is saved. Other
family members could not, and nor could the cloud company. Once something is saved at home it is
kept: deleting it from your phone does not remove it there, and only [Owner] can remove it."

16. "How do you feel about that?" (Let them talk. Then ask: "Is there a kind of thing you would
    want left out?")
17. "Have you ever had to decide whether to share something private with family? What did you
    do?" ★ (optional)

Record a few words of theme only (P12, P13). No quotes tied to names.

## 7. Help and habits (5 min)

18. "When something on your phone or computer goes wrong, who do you ask? Tell me about the last
    time." ★
19. "Who asks **you** for help?"
20. "Do you read e-mail? How often? Do you notice notifications on your phone, or do you turn
    them off?"

## 8. Language and accessibility (3 min; optional, gentle)

21. "Which language do you prefer for phone and computer screens?"
22. "Is anything about screens hard: small text, fiddly buttons, sounds you miss, or too many
    steps?" (Then look at the accessibility settings together: device checklist D13.)

## Close

"Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?" Thank them.

Then the observed tasks (E2, E5) follow, as in `../E1-S1/visit-plan.md`.

## Debrief (after **all** data collection for the visit, including E2/E5 tasks)

The interview deliberately does not comment on gaps, so that answers are not steered. Before
leaving, close the loop, so nobody is left worried or exposed:

1. Tell the person plainly what the check showed, without blame: for example "your phone photos
   are copied to iCloud, but the old laptop's photos exist only on that laptop".
2. If something important exists in **only one place**, offer the interim stopgap that E2-S3
   recommends for that device type (for example turning the phone's own cloud backup on, or
   copying a folder to a stick the owner takes home), or book a time to do it. Do not delete
   anything and do not buy storage on the spot.
3. Record on the person page, by **type only**, any single-copy finding and the action agreed. This
   is an interim-protection action list for the owner, not research data for export.
4. E1-S3 coding uses only the notes taken **before** the debrief, so the debrief does not
   contaminate the story counts.
